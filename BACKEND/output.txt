## Executive Summary

* A landlord may seek to recover possession of premises or evict a tenant or licensee by submitting a formal application to the Competent Authority under specific legal grounds [10].

* If an eviction order becomes final and the occupant fails to comply within thirty days, the Competent Authority or an authorized officer is legally empowered to evict the person and take forcible possession if necessary [1].

* When a court passes an eviction decree for repairs, the tenant must elect whether to re-occupy the premises, deliver possession by the specified date, and follow procedural requirements like depositing rent to protect their right of re-entry [5, 9].

* If a landlord fails to commence or complete authorized repairs without reasonable excuse after a tenant vacates, the tenant can apply to the court within one year for an order to restore possession [5].

## Detailed Answer

### Grounds and Procedures for Eviction
A landlord may submit an application to the Competent Authority to recover possession of premises from a tenant or licensee, notwithstanding other laws or contracts, provided it complies with specific statutory provisions [10]. If an eviction order is issued and remains uncomplied with for thirty days after becoming final, the Competent Authority or an authorized officer may physically evict the occupant, take possession, and deliver it to the landlord, using necessary force [1].

### Tenant Rights Regarding Repairs and Re-entry
When an eviction decree is granted for the purpose of repairs, the court must record whether the tenant elects to be placed back in occupation and specify a date for delivering possession [9]. 
- If the tenant delivers possession on time, the landlord must give notice two months before the anticipated completion of repairs [5, 9].
- Within thirty days of receiving that notice, the tenant must intimate acceptance of the accommodation and deposit one month's rent [5]. 
- If the tenant fails to provide this intimation and deposit, their right to occupy the premises terminates [5].

Conversely, if the landlord fails to start repairs within one month of the specified date, fails to complete them within a reasonable time, or fails to restore the tenant to occupation without a reasonable excuse, the tenant may apply to the court within one year of the specified date to obtain an order for vacant possession [5].
